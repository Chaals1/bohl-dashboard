How to use this

Two minutes: what to do, what the numbers mean, where they come from — and, just as honestly, what this tool can't know yet.

Start here

Using it in 30 seconds

  1. Pick a market up top — Phoenix, Austin, or Idaho.
  2. Sites are ranked best-fit first. The top of the list is where to look.
  3. Tap a site to open it — you'll see its fit %, the competition around it, traffic, who lives nearby, the broker's notes, and the brochure.
  4. Tick the compare box on 2–3 sites and hit "Compare" to line them up side by side; the best in each row is flagged.
  5. "Print / Save as PDF" on any site gives you a clean one-page sheet to email a broker.
  6. "Refresh" anytime pulls the newest rows straight from your brokers' sheets — no reload.
What everything means

The numbers, in plain English

  • Fit % — how well a site matches what Bohl looks for, read from the brochure and the broker's details. Think of it as a smart first read, not a verdict.
  • Confidence — how much of that fit % is backed by real info (a brochure, hard numbers) versus a thin listing. Low confidence = get a brochure before you lean on the score.
  • Nearest competitor + the map pins — the closest bowl/fast-casual spots (Cava, Chipotle, Qdoba, Sweetgreen), the traffic-magnet neighbors you want (In-N-Out, Chick-fil-A), and nearby gyms — each with the distance.
  • Traffic count — cars per day on that road, and whether the corner is quieter or busier than your other sites. It's context, not a score.
  • Income & population — the median household income and how many people live nearby, at 3, 5, and 10 miles.
  • Stage — where the deal sits, from Interested through Signed.
Where the numbers come from

Real sources, not guesses

  • Sites, prices, addresses, stage, notes → straight from each broker's own Google Sheet, live. Update the sheet, hit Refresh, it's here.
  • Competition → Google's live map data.
  • Traffic counts → public state traffic data.
  • Income & population → the U.S. Census — the real number, not a broker's "high-income area" line.
  • Fit % → an AI read of the site's brochure PDF plus the broker's details.
Straight talk

What this can't tell you (yet)

  • The fit % is a starting read, not a promise. It can't see what isn't in the brochure — the real HVAC or grease-trap situation, how flexible the landlord is, or how the space actually feels. Trust it less when confidence is low.
  • Traffic count is cars on the road, not customers in the door. Real foot-traffic (who actually walks in) is a pricey paid data source we've deliberately left out for now.
  • Income & population is the area around the pin, not yet your exact drive-time trade area. Close, but it's a circle on a map, not a 10-minute drive.
  • Competition is what Google lists. A brand-new spot that just opened might not show up yet.
  • Whether the rent is a good deal for the sales it'll do isn't calculated yet. That one needs your target numbers — give us your target sales and rent ceiling and we'll turn it on.
  • Prices and terms are whatever the broker typed. We show them; we don't independently verify them.
  • It's your brokers' data. If a sheet has a wrong or stray row, the dashboard will show it too — spot one, text me, and we'll get it flagged.
